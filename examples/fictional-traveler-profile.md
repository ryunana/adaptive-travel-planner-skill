# Fictional Traveler Profile Example

> This example is entirely fictional. It demonstrates useful specificity without representing a real person.

## Party and Luggage

- Two friends traveling with two cabin-size bags.
- Both can drive, but Traveler B avoids mountain roads.

## Schedule

- Natural wake time: 08:30-09:00.
- Realistic departure: 09:30.
- One major outdoor activity plus one nearby light activity per day.
- After a day above roughly 15,000 steps, prefer a late start or a mostly indoor day.

## Experience Preference

- Strong likes: coastal geology, industrial heritage, regional food markets, modern architecture.
- Strong dislikes: staged flower fields, costume photography streets, generic observation wheels.
- Will visit a theme park only for a distinctive ride or seasonal event unavailable nearby.
- Landscape and architecture photography; no portrait sessions.

## Physical and Health Boundaries

- Comfortable with four hours of mixed walking and breaks.
- Avoid rapid ascent above 2,500 meters.
- Traveler A gets motion sickness on small boats; ferries over 30 minutes require stable-weather checks and medication.

## Driving and Transport

- Comfortable daily driving: 2.5 hours.
- Conditional maximum: 5 hours on a pure transfer day, followed by a light morning.
- Avoid unpaved roads and night driving in unfamiliar mountains.
- Prefer rail between cities, rental car for rural coastlines, and transit in city centers.

## Accommodation

- Quiet room, reliable air conditioning, elevator, and recent cleanliness reviews are mandatory.
- Prefer two consecutive nights per hotel.
- Move hotels only to remove more than one hour of next-morning transfer or for a genuinely distinctive stay.

## Food

- Interested in seafood and regional breakfast.
- Traveler B has a shellfish allergy; restaurant recommendations must identify safe alternatives and cross-contact risk.
- Queue tolerance: 20 minutes; reservations preferred.
- A food detour is acceptable when it adds no more than 30 minutes door to door.

## Booking and Crowds

- Avoid timed tickets released more than two weeks in advance unless cancellation is easy.
- Low crowd tolerance.
- Will pay for a legitimate fast-access product, but not an unverified third-party tour bundle.

## Calibration Cases

| Experience | Actual score | Potential score | Conditions | What worked | What failed |
|---|---:|---:|---|---|---|
| Coastal cliff walk | 9 | 9 | Clear, windy, low crowd | Distinct geology and flexible route | Final shuttle wait |
| Restored warehouse district | 7 | 8 | Light rain | Architecture and indoor galleries | Repetitive retail section |
| Famous flower park | 4 | 6 | Weekend crowd | One seasonal garden | Staged sets and long queue |

## Planning Output Preference

- Compare three options and recommend one.
- Always include weather threshold, actual transport, walking load, and a cancellation point.
- Explicitly say when a famous attraction is not worth the detour.
